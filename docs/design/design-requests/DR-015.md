# DR-015 — Three of entry 7's five stat cards have no source in the product

Status: **drafted** — 2026-08-29 (D-114). Not sent. The ready-to-paste prompt is
the last section.

**Blocks nothing.** The screen is built on the facts the product holds, and the
stat row stays inside `template-overview-stats.md` §1's 3–5 floor.

## The finding, in one sentence

`SPEC/manifest.md` §7 names the stats **Views · Visitors · Avg. time · Bounce ·
Agent hits**, and three of those five are not merely unmeasured — they are
**unmeasurable from what Klytos stores**, for reasons that are the product's
design rather than an oversight.

## What the product actually records

`AnalyticsManager::recordPageView()` writes exactly six fields per pageview
(`installer/core/analytics-manager.php:102-110`):

```
page_path · referrer_domain · device_category · visitor_hash · date · timestamp
```

That is the whole record. Nothing else about a visit is kept anywhere, and the
file's own header states why: no cookies, no fingerprinting, no user agent, no
raw IP, no cross-session behaviour.

## The three, one at a time

### *Avg. time* — there is one instant, and a duration needs two

A pageview stores a single `timestamp`. There is no session, no exit event, no
second pageview linked to the first, and nothing that observes a person leaving.
Time-on-page is the distance between two instants and the product holds one.

**This is the identical shape as entry 18's *Settlement lag* (DR-014):** a
measurement whose second point does not exist.

### *Bounce* — there is no session to have one pageview in

A bounce is defined on a SESSION: a visit with exactly one pageview. Klytos has
no session concept in analytics at all. `visitor_hash` groups by visitor-DAY (see
below), which is not a visit, and nothing records a visit boundary.

The nearest computable thing — "visitor-days with exactly one pageview" — is a
**different metric wearing a standard metric's name**, which is worse than an
absent card: a reader would take it for the industry figure and it is not.

### *Agent hits* — the only agent record in the product is a PAYMENT record

`recordPageView()` stores no user agent, deliberately, as one of the privacy
guarantees the analytics engine is built on. The only place Klytos records an
agent is `X402\TransactionLog`, which logs `bot_user_agent` **on a transaction**
— i.e. agents that PAID. An unpaid agent hit is recorded nowhere.

So an "Agent hits" card fed from x402 would be entry 18's *Paid requests* under
another name, on a different screen — the duplication D-090 refused — and it
would not answer the question the card's name asks.

## A fourth thing, which is smaller and is a wording question

The *Visitors* card **is** built, but it does not count what a reader will assume.
`hashVisitorIdentity()` is SHA-256 of the IP plus a salt that **rotates daily**,
by design, so a visitor cannot be followed across days. Over a 30-day range the
figure is therefore the count of distinct **visitor-days**: one person visiting on
ten days counts ten times.

The build has kept §7's label and added a supporting line on the card stating what
the number measures. That is a stopgap, not a decision: the label is Design's.

## What the build has done in the meantime, stated exactly

Four cards, all measured:

| Card | Source |
|---|---|
| Views | `total_views` |
| Visitors | `unique_visitors`, with the visitor-day note |
| Avg. views/day | `total_views` ÷ the range, `—` and not `0` over no traffic |
| Pages tracked | distinct paths in the range |

*Avg. views/day* and *Pages tracked* are **shipped product** — both were on this
screen before the redesign — so they are kept rather than dropped, which is also
what keeps the row inside §1's 3–5. Logged as adaptation 97. Two cards alone
would break the template's own floor.

## What Design needs to decide

1. **Do *Avg. time*, *Bounce* and *Agent hits* stay in §7?** If they do, they are
   product capabilities with their own storage, and each needs a decision about
   what Klytos is willing to record — sessions and exit events are precisely the
   tracking this engine was built not to do. If they do not, §7's stat list wants
   rewriting against what a cookie-free analytics engine can honestly answer.
2. **What should the four built cards be called?** *Avg. views/day* and *Pages
   tracked* are the build's wording for shipped figures the manifest does not name.
3. **Is "Visitors" the right label for a visitor-day count**, or should the card
   say what it measures — and if the label stays, is a supporting line the right
   place for the caveat?
